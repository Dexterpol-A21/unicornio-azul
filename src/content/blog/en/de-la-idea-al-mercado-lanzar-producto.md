Having a product idea is not the same as having a business. Between the two there is a journey most people do not see until they walk it: validate, industrialize, certify, import and sell.

At Unicornio Azul we have walked the whole journey. The clearest example is Roll Order: it arrived as a patent document and reached the market as a product selling on Amazon, with a presence at CES in Las Vegas. This article explains what it really takes to launch your own product, without shortcuts and without fluff.

## Why most products never reach the market

The number is brutal: according to Clayton Christensen, professor at Harvard Business School, around 30,000 new products are launched every year and 95% fail. And the cause is almost never the product itself, but the path to get it out.

A Shopify study goes further: 85% of teams admit that sales, marketing and development are out of sync, and 95% of launches miss their revenue goals. The failure is not in the factory. It is in the process.

> A product without a journey is an idea with an invoice. The value is in connecting the whole path, not in a single task.

## What it takes to launch a product

Launching a product is not an event, it is a process in three phases: pre-launch, launch and post-launch. If you skip one, you pay for it later.

### Phase 1. Pre-launch: validate before manufacturing

Before spending on molds, stock or factories, you have to answer one question: does anyone want to buy this? The sources in this phase are the same ones we use to validate any business idea: real demand, interviews, competition and an MVP.

But with a physical product there is something more: technical and economic viability. One thing is for the idea to be liked, another is for it to be manufacturable at a cost that leaves margin. In Roll Order, before producing we validated what the market asked for (color, connectors) by analysing real sales data on Amazon, across six countries.

### Phase 2. Industrialization: from prototype to manufacturable product

This is where the idea becomes something that can be produced at scale. It involves decisions you do not see at first glance:

- **Who manufactures and where**: evaluating factories, real capacity and reliability.
- **Molds and tooling**: the hidden cost that brings the most surprises.
- **Certifications**: CE, RoHS and the category-specific ones. Without them, the product does not enter most markets.
- **Real costs**: production, packaging, freight, tariffs and taxes.

In Roll Order, the mold was secured for a fraction of the quote the client had found on their own. That is the kind of difference that managing this phase well makes.

### Phase 3. Go-to-market: channel, listing and projection

A finished product does not sell itself. It needs a channel. For most physical products today, that channel is Amazon or a marketplace, but there are also decisions about brand, price and positioning.

According to ATTN Agency, a countdown of just 4 weeks before launch delivers 34% higher day-one conversion, and reaching 8 weeks drives 89% more social engagement. Rushing, again, is expensive.

In Roll Order, the launch included the Amazon listing, the first campaigns and international projection: the product was presented in the innovation zone at CES Las Vegas, alongside the client, supporting the commercial management.

## The full journey, at a glance

From idea to market there are five links. If one fails, the chain breaks:

1. **Patent or idea** — protected, but with no product yet.
2. **Industrialization** — factory, molds and viable costs.
3. **Market** — certification, import and sales channel.
4. **Projection** — brand, price and international visibility.

The value is not in one link, but in connecting all of them. Whoever only designs does not launch. Whoever only manufactures does not sell. The result appears when the journey is complete.

## What you can apply to your product today

If you have a product idea, do not start by manufacturing. Start with these three questions:

- Is there real demand or just intuition?
- Can it be manufactured at a cost that leaves margin?
- Through which channel will you sell, and who will buy it?

When you have answers backed by data, not opinions, you will be ready for the next step. Until then, every euro you invest is a blind bet.
