People searching how much it costs to create a brand are usually ready to spend. The problem is that almost nobody tells them what they are buying. A logo is not a brand. Filing a name is not launching a product. And the budget that matters is the one that lets you sell, not the one that leaves you with a pretty PDF.

If you want a single number, there is none. If you want a real budget, you have to split three layers: protect the name, build the identity, and put the business on the market.

## The cheap part: filing the trademark

Filing is not creating. It is protecting. And the official fees are public.

In 2026, Spain’s [OEPM](https://www.oepm.es/es/tasas-y-precios-publicos/tasas-de-marcas-y-nombres-comerciales/) charges, from 1 April, €127.88 for the first class if you file electronically, and €82.84 for each extra class. A national mark covers Spain and lasts 10 years, renewable.

If you sell across several EU countries, an EU trade mark at EUIPO costs €850 for the first class, €50 for the second, and €150 for each class from the third. One title covers all 27 member states. Once the application is published, third parties have three months to oppose: paying the fee does not close the file.

If you use an agent, add search and filing fees. In Spain the usual range is about €400–700 for a well-handled national mark and €1,350–1,750 for an EU one. It is still the cheapest part of the project.

> Filing a name for €128 does not give you a brand. It gives you a title. The business starts afterwards.

## The visible part: naming, identity and system

This is where the “brand” quotes you see on Google live. There is no official rate card: these are orders of magnitude from studio work, not a tariff. In practice, a basic identity (logo, colour, type, a few applications) often sits between €1,500 and €3,500. A new brand with a system and first applications, between €3,500 and €8,000. If positioning, tone and a full identity with guidelines are in scope, the work moves toward €8,000–18,000.

Those ranges help you compare. They do not tell you if the project is viable. A name you cannot register gets thrown away. An identity without positioning gets copied. A brand book nobody uses does not sell a single unit.

The useful question is not “how much does a logo cost?”. It is “does this identity help someone pay more, choose you first, or remember you later?”. If not, you are buying decoration.

## The expensive part: actually launching

If your own brand sits on a physical product, branding is one line in the budget, not the budget. What really moves money is:

- Prototype, engineering and tooling.
- First production run and minimum stock.
- Certifications (CE, RoHS and category-specific ones).
- Import, duties, freight and warehousing.
- Channel: Amazon, your own site, retail, or all three. On Amazon Spain, referral fees for most categories sit between 8% and 15%.
- Import VAT at 21% if the goods enter from outside the EU: it is recoverable, but you have to front it.
- Ads, listing and the first months of traction.

That is what people are really asking when they search how much it costs to launch a product. There is no agency rate card for this: cost comes from the product, the factory, the volume and the channel. On Roll Order, the mould was secured for a fraction of the quote the client had found on their own. That difference is not in the logo. It is in how you industrialise.

## Three budgets, not one

To stop mixing oranges with moulds, use three boxes:

**1. Legal brand.** OEPM or EUIPO fees, clearance search, classes. Hundreds or a few thousand euros.

**2. Perceived brand.** Naming, identity, tone, pack and digital applications. From a few thousand to around ten, depending on scope.

**3. Brand in the market.** A manufacturable product, stock, certifications, logistics and channel. This is where most of the money goes, and where a project stands or falls.

If you only quote box 2, the number looks nice and the business is half-built. If you quote all three, you know whether you can pay for it.

## What not to spend on first

Do not spend €20,000 on a rebrand if you have not [validated demand](/en/ideas/como-validar-una-idea-de-negocio-antes-de-invertir/). Do not spend on a spectacular website if you have no product cost or margin. Do not spend on 5,000 units if nobody has reserved the first one.

The order is the same as always: market, numbers, then identity. [Defining the business model](/en/what-we-do/business-development) comes before the brand book.
