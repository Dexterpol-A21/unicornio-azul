Creating a brand from scratch does not start in Illustrator. It starts with a sentence the customer can repeat: what you give them, why you are different, and why they should pay you. The logo comes later. If you get to the logo first, you are decorating a promise that does not exist yet.

> It is not a logo. It is the promise you make to your customer.

That is the line we use at Unicornio Azul when we talk about [building a brand from scratch](/en/what-we-do/business-development). Naming, identity and positioning are not three separate deliverables. They are the same decision, told three ways.

## Positioning first

Before the name, answer three things with data, not taste:

- **Who** you are talking to. An Amazon shopper is not a B2B purchasing director.
- **Who** you compete against. If you cannot name three alternatives, you do not have a market, you have an idea.
- **Why you**. Price, specialisation, channel, origin, guarantee. Pick one and drop the rest.

Positioning is the promise. Everything else translates it. If this part stays vague, the name will be clever and forgettable, and the identity will be pretty and generic.

A common mistake: copying the look of the category and calling that “professional”. Christensen, Cook and Hall saw it with Nielsen: looking different does not predict whether a launch works. Customers do not choose you because you look like everyone else, or because the pack is stranger. They choose you because, in five seconds, they understand what they gain by staying with you.

## Then naming

A good name is not the one the room likes most. It is the one you can say, spell, register and defend.

Check, in this order:

1. **It is understood and pronounced** in the languages where you will sell.
2. **It is free** at the OEPM and, if you sell outside Spain, at EUIPO. Same for the domain and social handles.
3. **It is not so descriptive** that you cannot extend the line tomorrow.
4. **It does not promise** what the product cannot deliver.

At EUIPO, once the application is published, third parties have three months to oppose. A name that “sounds free” in a quick search can still be blocked later, with the pack already in motion.

Naming is cheap to throw away if you do it early, and very expensive if you do it with printed packs and a live listing. That is why it sits next to the trademark search, not at the end of design.

## Then identity

Identity is the system through which the promise is seen: logo, colour, type, pack, site, Amazon listing, trade fair. A standalone logotype is not a system. A system is a set of rules someone can apply without asking you every Friday.

Three practical tests:

- **Readable at 16 mm** on a pack and as a favicon on a phone.
- **Distinct at three metres** from competitors on a shelf or in search.
- **Consistent** between the physical product and the digital channel. If the pack says one thing and the listing another, you do not have a brand: you have two messages.

Identity follows positioning. If you promise industrial rigour, do not design like a meditation app. If you promise accessibility, do not design like a luxury watch.

## How it fits SORIE™

At Unicornio Azul we do not split the brand from the rest of the journey. The [SORIE™ methodology](/en/sorie-methodology) sets the order: strategy, opportunity, reality, innovation and execution.

- **Strategy**: the promise and who it is for.
- **Opportunity**: whether the market already looks for this or you are inventing it.
- **Reality**: costs, factory, certifications, margin.
- **Innovation**: what you actually do differently, not in the slogan.
- **Execution**: pack, channel, listing, fair, sales.

A brand from scratch that ignores reality (price, mould, duty, Amazon) is a costume. It falls over on the first order.

## Mistakes that make the fix expensive

- Falling in love with a name you cannot register.
- Paying for a full identity before [validating demand](/en/ideas/como-validar-una-idea-de-negocio-antes-de-invertir/).
- Confusing “create your own brand” with “make a logo and a website”.
- Changing the look every time the designer changes.
- Promising “premium” with a product cost that cannot hold the price.

Fixing a badly planted brand costs more than planting it well. Especially if you already have stock, tooling or a listing with reviews.

## Where to start today

If you are creating your own brand now, do not open a logo contest. Write on one page:

1. The promise in one sentence.
2. The customer in one sentence.
3. Three competitors and what you will not compete on.
4. Three name candidates, with a trademark search done.
5. How that will look on the pack or the listing, not on a moodboard.

When those five lines hold, the identity has ground. Until then, every euro in design is work you may have to throw away.
